**Hadal · Hydrothermal vents** (`?level=vents`) is a ten-screen / 100-second stage. Modular overhead rock and vent cards occupy near banks, middle terraces and distant ledges. Their world depths, inward offsets and progressively darker water tints make the layers visible through gaps in the foreground. Low near shelves sit outboard, with the middle and deep terraces stepping inward to form a V-shaped cross-section. Chimney cards have raised upper sections and low rubble feet; their stage-view projection preserves the painted silhouette while keeping vent mouths in front of base-level shelves. The rough, fractured basalt seabed lies below every middle/distant formation; only the final stretch rises into a solid cliff lip. Mineral plumes and suspended particles animate across those depths. The floor ends at an actual cliff over an abyss. An eight-second quiet approach carries the camera over a curved lip and lowers the view into a circular shaft. The near shelf passes below the camera before the head-on fight; a continuous cylindrical inner wall extends more than 70 units down, with no opaque floor across the opening. Horizontal floor cards pass below the camera; fixed vertical cards form the far rim. **Barnacle Sovereign**, a heavily encrusted articulated raster crab, crawls down into view from high on that same inner wall after the five-second camera move settles, finishing its descent at 107 seconds with no opacity fade or scenery reset. Eight two-link walking legs brace on the visible inner rock face, while independently hinged crusher/cutter claws, eye stalks and split armor plates use overlapping, depth-ordered sprite cards. Extra barnacle colonies ride the shell and moving joints. During the fight it scurries sideways in bursts around the shaft, pauses, and reverses direction. Stance feet hold world anchors while swing feet lift and step; the view follows most of the angular travel so the cylindrical wall scrolls in response. The ship and controls stay fixed to the combat plane, while moving joints and gills retain their weapon targets. Break the glowing claw joints (12 HP each); broken claws slump but remain attached. Destroying both opens the central gill chamber (40 HP), for 64 total HP. Phase damage is capped at the current joint, and old ship shots cannot carry a claw hit into a newly exposed target. Claws release the existing locked three-shot warnings; the exposed gill chamber expands the pattern to five. Ship, drone, wing, Towline and Pulse weapons all work through both claw phases and the core. Weakpoint markers, laser contact/ablation and recoil follow the posed joints and gills.

Hadal afterburners shed fine, turbulent bubble trails lasting 0.30–0.62 seconds from the ship, drone and equipped wing pods. Bubbles scatter at varied speeds and drift independently after emission, expand, fade and freeze on pause. Hot water above vent mouths refracts the scene through a single bounded, downsampled capture with depth rejection to protect foreground actors. The capture and particle buffers warm before launch.

Small, muted groups of background mollusks reuse the barnacle atlas foot and paired shell plates on low near and middle shelves. They breathe gently, remain rooted in world space, and have no weapons or collision. The crab is 12% larger, with matching weakpoint positions and feet that still brace on the shaft wall.

Eighteen articulated trench-shrimp patrols, six scenery-fixed barnacle mortars and four burrow-worm ambushers populate the route, with repeated drops of every wing accessory. Shrimp weave in groups, cross in pairs and follow mirrored single-file loops with continuous headings. Their overlapping abdominal plates, flexible joints, tail fans, antennae and two-link limbs animate independently. They have 3 HP, award 125 points and fire a narrow pair of aimed shots every 3.1 seconds. Burrow worms hide in low mineral holes, emerge over a 0.9-second warning with a fixed attack direction, and lunge across the lane with a long, connected muscular body and opening jaws. A faint curved warning traces the committed path. They hold the bite briefly, then retract into the hole; attacks repeat every 6.6 seconds. Worms have 8 HP and award 250 points. Their entire exposed length deals contact damage and takes weapon damage; contact does not kill the worm. Concealed flesh has no aimed-fire or contact hitboxes. Worms emit no projectiles. Fixed enemies spawn three seconds before their original arrival, beyond the top edge; their attack schedules are unchanged. Worm flesh renders above its hole. Ordered polygon depth offsets keep overlapping body, collar and jaw sprites stable; the hole stays open after defeat, without a cover. A defeated worm leaves a dim gray hole attached to the scenery for the rest of the scrolling route; restarting restores the enemies. Their living joints and the rotated shrimp bodies share swept projectile, laser, ramming and Towline bounds; decorative tube rims and fine limbs are not extra targets.

Each mortar has a fixed cemented foot, independently opening shell leaves, a tracking siphon and a recoiling aperture. A 0.8-second wind-up precedes each three-pearl volley; volleys repeat every 3.6 seconds. Mortars have 4 HP and award 150 points. All weapons damage them throughout the cycle. Their bases share world anchors with their supporting scenery. The raster formations and cliff are decorative; movement, firing and hit geometry remain on the stable combat plane throughout the camera swing. The final boss pose agrees with the original cannon collision and laser-contact geometry.

**Io · Caldera** (`?level=io`) is a **ten-screen / 100-second production route** with eighteen Skipper patrols and five Crucible crossings, repeat drops of every wing accessory, an eight-second camera approach, and the **Caldera Engine** fight. Select it in the launch menu; Frostwake remains the default.

Crucibles are heavy soot-black spherical carriers with ceramic cheeks and opening pouring valves. They cross from alternating sides, visibly charge the valve for 0.7 seconds, and release three aimed molten droplets every 2.6 seconds. Droplets cool into dark, cracked slag over 0.9 seconds and slow down; both hot and cold slag can be shot apart. Carriers have 10 HP and award 300 points, slag has 1 HP and awards 10. The first and third carriers drop a cannon and Omega respectively. Every weapon can destroy both, including laser, piercing Omega, Towline and Pulse. Their models and cooling shaders are pooled and prewarmed; both appear in the Vite workshop. Existing reviewed textures remain optional.

The route develops from sulfur outskirts (0–20 s), through extraction terraces (20–40 s), processing yards (40–60 s), and dense refinery clusters (60–78 s), to a molten breach (78–90 s) and the broken caldera rim (90–100 s). Its layout follows the reviewed progression mockups: domed pressure vessels on grated, supported terraces, branching insulated pipes, quarry conveyors, furnaces, ruined towers, a damaged pipe gantry across the breach, then cable winches and a cooled lava spill at the rim. The basalt corridor makes broad S-bends between steep sulfur-stained banks, with machinery, pipe branches and conveyors following the changing route. All structures remain below the aircraft; scenery is decorative and cannot block, damage or reward the player.

Conveyor ribs scroll at the same speed as their jagged yellow sulfur loads. Transfer yards occupy gaps between vessel clusters, with reserved clearance around the belts and terminals that also excludes large rocks. Filled feeder hoppers cover the loading positions; covered receiving bins conceal the final drop and loop reset. Furnace vents pulse. Registered burner jets use a two-piece view-aligned plane: an always-rounded lower half at the mouth, and an upper half crossfading from a rising side flame to a matching semicircular cap near overhead. Both halves share looping turbulence and meet at one diameter. Camera-facing smoke puffs follow bounded ballistic arcs from the actual exhaust mouths. Mip-filtered thermal detail flows across both the molten breach and caldera lava, beneath separate cooled crust slabs and glowing fracture walls. These effects all use simulation time, freeze when paused or at results, and restart from the same initial state. They allocate no new geometry or textures during play.

A settled-dust pass adds sulfur powder and loose grains beneath the conveyor transfer points, dusty service aprons, and darker fallout around refinery clusters. Deposits conform to the terrain; dust and irregular vertical grime remain fixed to painted and metal surfaces as the scene scrolls and turns. The PBR coating dulls reflections and raises roughness while preserving furnace emissions and target markings. Chimney mouths have charred metal, exhaust is muted gray-brown, and low powder puffs drift close to the transfer yards. All masks and grains are generated at runtime, with no new uploaded assets or collision changes.

The continuous 3D heightfield uses denser, irregular triangulation along the banks and sparse sampling farther away, divided into culled chunks with identical boundary heights and normals. Broken talus and half-buried fragments roughen the shoulders. Blended Cartesian albedo/bump projections prevent concentric cap mapping on silos and rocks; the silo paint band occupies its own segment of one tapered shell, avoiding overlapping panels. Rock volumes and rubble are instanced, fixed industrial details are merged per assembly/material, and animated ore, jets and smoke use shared instanced batches. The world-space shadow sun stays anchored to the terrain texel grid through scrolling and camera pitch. Lava/furnace fill and a finite HDR reflection probe give machinery warm reflected light. Everything is created and prewarmed before launch.

The boss and its six foot foundations share that same world. Perspective is active from launch. Stage flight keeps a fixed oblique angle and a straight heading, with no path-following pan, bank or pitch sway. Aircraft controls and weapon positions stay anchored to the screen. The ship then coasts to a hover while the view blends smoothly into the existing 35° boss framing above the ground, with no scenic image replacement or boss reveal fade. The 72-HP **Caldera Engine** retains its eight-scoop dredging wheel, locked three-shot volleys and support-break sequence: the first 20 damage breaks the cyan coupling, articulates one failed leg and sags the chassis while the feet remain fixed. A shutter opens over the central machinery for the remaining 52 HP. All ship, drone, wing, Towline and Pulse weapons work in both phases; projectile origins, impact markers and laser contacts follow the posed target.

Vite dev offers **Launch at transition**, the **Io camera study** (`/io-study.html`) with a 0–108 s timeline and section jump menu, failed-support inspection, and Skipper/Crucible/slag/Caldera/burner asset-workshop entries. Production has no QA hooks or skip controls. The owner's reviewed basalt and ceramic maps are optional; finite mip-filtered procedural textures preserve the full route and combat if media is absent, missing or corrupt. Generated media continues to go through chat for owner review/upload.

**Titan · Research station** (`?level=titan`) uses a ten-screen ground plate and begins its ascent at 84 seconds, six seconds before the end of the original ground route. Twenty insect encounters carry repeat drops of all four wing accessories. Smaller four-winged cicadas fly in coherent chevrons, mirrored crossing sweeps, and staggered accelerating dives. Five centipedes grow from six to eight individually damageable, overlapping body sections along the route. Destroying a middle section leaves two connected fragments that turn smoothly and scurry toward opposite sides; cut ends expose a dark joint rather than growing another head. Surviving sections retain their weapon targets, health, and drops. Further cuts, ramming, ship/drone fire, homing missiles, piercing omega shots, continuous lasers, Towline, and Pulse all use the same section hitboxes. Only intact centipede heads shoot; fleeing fragments stop firing and leave the playfield. The patrol models share geometry/materials and are pooled and warmed before launch, including severed ends, so encounters need no new media or first-use GPU uploads. A seven-second ascent follows the patrol: the station and layered horizontal cloud surfaces share a world-space height/pitch transform as the ship climbs and pitches into a face-on fight with **Titan Cicada**. The boss physically rises ahead and enters the camera frustum without a reveal fade or depth slide. Mirrored ground extensions and procedural side ridges cover the exposed terrain; peaks project through the haze during the fight. The three cloud surfaces have moving geometric relief, slope-based lighting, and denser tessellation near the flight corridor. Relief tapers at the distant horizon and stays below the lowest boss-fight aircraft position. The reference-informed head has a broad dark brow, smaller nub-mounted eyes, a downward-pointing ocellar triangle clear of the lower face, a continuous pear-shaped clypeus with swept ribs and a central seam, and a short folded rostrum. Its four translucent wings have veins clipped to their actual membrane triangles, its compound-eyed head sways, and six spined legs move while alternating cyan thorax vents charge locked three-shot volleys. The boss has 80 HP, warning rings before firing, and supports every drone/wing weapon and laser contact/shield effect. In Vite dev, **Launch at transition** skips the patrol and starts at the beginning of the ascent. The uploaded `public/encounters/titan-research-ground-10screens.png` scrolls once, from the outer sampling grounds at its bottom to the disrupted core at its top; the central route remains intact, with mirrored extensions covering the surrounding ground during ascent. Selected ground lamps blink independently and cyan contents move through the glass pipe. The river stays static. Stronger drifting amber haze, windblown dust, and cooling exhaust from registered machinery vents add motion above the ground. An optional four-part raster atlas supplies maintenance crawlers with rigidly rotating survey turrets and two-link tool arms. Crawlers stay hidden until their reviewed atlas is uploaded; gameplay and the remaining scenery work without it. These elements are decorative; they cannot damage, block, or reward the player. Atmosphere stays below the aircraft and combat graphics, and all motion freezes with the simulation. Patrol wings attach to dorsal hinges and their full beat stays above the carapace. Choose a level before launch. **Frostwake · Ice field** is the default: a ten-screen route with a 90-second traversal, twenty authored waves, repeat weapon drops, and the mosasaur boss **Leviathan**. Ichthyosaur hunting packs and four-flipper plesiosaur sweepers replace the borrowed scout/raptor roster. Hunters alternate weaving chevrons, paired pincers, delayed slalom trains and five-member lines that complete a full loop before diving out; later loops reverse direction. The ten sections move through open drift, ice passages, survey wreckage, and a clear boss arena. The sector readout shows progress from 1/10 to 10/10. Use `?level=ice` to select it directly.

The hunters have shallow lateral nostrils near the beak base, scaleless, countershaded bodies and bank to reveal their pale flanks before accelerating. Plesiosaurs keep a substantial neck, a steady torso, coordinated four-flipper strokes with embedded shoulder/hip roots, transverse stripes, smooth body skin, localized flipper scales, and no dorsal fin; they release a slower five-shot mouth fan. Their long bodies have their own projectile/contact bounds, and authored carriers still release wing accessories. Leviathan has a muscular body with irregular mottled spots, small scale detail, lateral tail propulsion, a vertical two-lobed fluke, and an articulated jaw. The angular skull has raised cranial/brow ridges and forward-facing recessed eyes behind protective orbital rims. A single connected blubber mesh runs from the torso through the neck into the skull and chin; the ventral neck stretches smoothly with the hinged jaw. The socket backs extend into the skull. Lit, muted red flesh covers the palate, throat and jaw-bound tongue; aperture-dependent cavity occlusion approximates light blocked by the roof and cheeks, keeping the deep maw dark while exposing warmer flesh at the lip and tongue. Subdued bone-colored teeth frame the bite. Gentle head yaw, pitch and roll reveal the sides during the fight; the mouth marker and laser contact follow the posed head. Its 60-HP fight uses a central mouth target and locked, alternating horizontal/vertical three-shot volleys with a longer dodge interval. The jaw, charge glow, mouth marker, hit feedback, and laser contact follow the posed rig. Ordinary ship fire remains continuous. The marine projectiles are amber ice-like pellets; these attacks and survival in space are fictional.

The models are procedural skinned meshes with shared geometry and materials. Body and flipper lofts have closed ends, including the plesiosaur nose and tail. Hunter facing, bank, muzzle positions and capsule hit bounds follow their formation paths through sideways and reversed turns. Bone-driven tails, necks, head and flippers are deterministic and pause with the flight. Laser contact tests the posed vertices rather than the bind-pose silhouette. Every pooled model and skeleton buffer is primed before launch; animated bone matrices stream into existing GPU storage during play. The dev workshop includes the boss and patrol cicadas, a centipede with a scrubbed middle-section break and opposite escapes, hunter, sweeper, and Leviathan with animation scrubbing and orbit views.

Anatomy follows the earlier mockups, informed by [scaleless skin, blubber and countershading in *Stenopterygius*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0775-x), [smooth tail skin and small flipper scales in one Jurassic plesiosaur](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2025.01.001), and [a preserved bilobed mosasaur tail fin](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms3423). These specimens inform the designs; exact colors, scale distribution beyond preserved areas, behavior and proportions remain artistic interpretations.

Frostwake is assembled from six shared procedural asteroid shapes, wreckage modules, and anchored mining stations. Irregular fracture planes retain sharp rims while worn regions and UV seams share smooth normals. Periodic cellular normal and roughness maps add angular joints, tilted ice faces, and chipped frost. A nonmetallic physical material uses water ice's 1.31 refractive index, with a shared procedural HDR lighting probe for white specular glints. Fake refraction bends the same cropped, rotating galaxy behind the clear faces; frosted joints scatter more light. Three bounded half-resolution depth layers let clear ice refract other asteroids behind it. Stable body IDs reject self-refraction and depth tests reject foreground leakage. Capture colors retain sky transmission without recursive framebuffer feedback; actors and combat effects stay out of the buffers. Overlaps deeper than the captured layers fall back to the galaxy. The relief maps and lighting probe are included in preflight warm-up. Background rocks, solid outcrops, and foreground rocks have distinct real depths. Free asteroids have independent visible tumbles (0.07–0.18 radians per second) about arbitrary 3D axes; only outcrops with mining stations and attitude jets hold a fixed lane orientation. Small survey huts give way to weathered gantries, container decks, refineries, and docks as the route progresses. Metal surfaces carry procedural grime and minor corrosion. During the boss approach, the field pitches into a 3D corridor along the same forward axis as the fighter and its wing pods. Leviathan paces the ship as rocks and industry stream out of the distance throughout the fight. Decorative rocks recycle beyond the viewport so the pursuit never runs out of scenery; solid stations pass only once and the arena stays clear. A one-and-a-half-screen galactic plate fills the distant sky and reorients during the camera turn. Repeat-aware edge blending hides wrap seams and covers every viewport corner. Nothing uses a mirrored or repeating long backdrop. Pale outcrops with paired amber markers obstruct the fighter, drone, and wing pods without damaging hull; the central escape lane stays open and the last screen has no solid outcrops. Enemy paths stay clear of the solids. Dark distant rocks and unmarked foreground fragments are decorative. Layout is deterministic and shared with collision, so optional media cannot change the route.
