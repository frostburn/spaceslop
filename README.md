# Spaceslop

Vibe-coded vertical shooter for the browser (PWA), with production-length ice and Titan routes and selectable preserve and ant hive prototype levels, scrolling waves, a camera transition, and a head-on boss fight.

## Play locally

Use Node.js 22.12 or newer.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Tap or click the playfield to send the drone to that point. It parks and fires automatically. Drag from the same touch to steer the ship while the drone covers its lane. On a keyboard use WASD or the arrow keys. Guns fire automatically. Use Pulse or Space to clear incoming fire, and Pause or Escape to pause. In the boss fight, line up horizontally to lock on with the ship. Put the drone on the green target marker (Leviathan’s mouth, or a cannon on the other bosses), and keep the ship clear of amber warning rings. The other bosses switch exposed cannons after each volley.

In Vite dev mode, **Launch at boss** starts the selected level at its normal approach transition with your chosen pilot, drone and test wing loadout. The shortcut is absent from production builds; ordinary **Launch** always starts the scrolling stage.

Choose **Sentry** or **Towline** before launch. With Towline equipped, park the drone and drag the ship to sweep the arc through enemy formations. The arc pulses automatically while the drone is parked. Against the boss, sweep the line across the green target during a pulse.

Enemies can drop wing weapons. Fly through a pickup to equip a pod that flies alongside your ship. An empty slot takes priority, and with both occupied the pickup replaces the pod on the side where you collected it. For testing, expand **Test wing loadout** before launch to equip either wing immediately. The picker is available in Vite dev and on builds opened with `?testLoadout=1`.

- **Homing Cannon** fires weak guided missiles.
- **Standard Cannon** fires straight down its lane.
- **Continuous Laser** builds damage while it stays on one target.
- **Omega Cannon** charges slowly and fires a heavy bolt through multiple enemies.

During boss fights, line a wing up horizontally with the green target. Homing missiles steer toward it automatically.

Each gun has its own projectile silhouette and trail: cyan ship darts, mint drone pulses, finned homing missiles, cannon tracers, and gold omega lances. Enemy plasma and hive spores have bright centers and dark rims. Lasers ripple along their length and burn an ablation flame that sheds incandescent sparks at contact. Regular enemies use their projected opaque silhouette; bosses use the active muzzle's animated contact anchor, including recoil. Laser damage produces no extra body-hit bursts. Bullet and other impact effects still appear, including when they hit a target under laser fire. Damage feedback renders over enemy shells and mold.

Ineffective boss laser hits show expanding blue shield ripples at the beam endpoint. Ablation flames and sparks appear only while the beam deals damage.

Wing pickups use metal badges with matching colors and distinct weapon icons, rotating beacons, and a short arrival animation. The homing icon is a fixed crosshair with two shrinking lock-on circles. Muzzle flashes and collection bursts give immediate feedback. These effects use shared geometry and pooled instances, are warmed before launch, and need no new asset files.

**Hadal · Hydrothermal vents** (`?level=vents`) is a ten-screen / 100-second prototype route. Modular overhead rock and vent cards occupy near banks, middle terraces and distant ledges. Their world depths, inward offsets and progressively darker water tints make the layers visible through gaps in the foreground. Low near shelves sit outboard, with the middle and deep terraces stepping inward to form a V-shaped cross-section. Chimney cards have raised upper sections and low rubble feet; their stage-view projection preserves the painted silhouette while keeping vent mouths in front of base-level shelves. The rough, fractured basalt seabed lies below every middle/distant formation; only the final stretch rises into a solid cliff lip. Mineral plumes and suspended particles animate across those depths. The floor ends at an actual cliff over an abyss. An eight-second quiet approach coasts past the edge and smoothly changes the view from overhead to head-on. Horizontal floor cards pass below the camera; fixed vertical cards form the far rim. The prototype guardian climbs in an arc around that rim, with no opacity fade or scenery reset. Its six simple support limbs are a staging proxy for the eventual crustacean rig. Combat uses the original alternating-cannon prototype boss, with all weapons and normal results/restart supported.

Eighteen prototype scout waves and six scenery-fixed barnacle mortars populate the route, with repeated drops of every wing accessory. Each mortar has a fixed cemented foot, independently opening shell leaves, a tracking siphon and a recoiling aperture. A 0.8-second wind-up precedes each three-pearl volley; volleys repeat every 3.6 seconds. Mortars have 4 HP and award 150 points. All weapons damage them throughout the cycle. Their bases share world anchors with their supporting scenery. The raster formations and cliff are decorative; movement, firing and hit geometry remain on the stable combat plane throughout the camera swing. The final boss pose agrees with the original cannon collision and laser-contact geometry.

The optional transparent atlas goes at `public/encounters/vent-formations.png`. Use **four columns × two rows** of equal-sized cells, retaining alpha and the cell order below. A 2:1 PNG around 1792×896 or 2048×1024 is suitable. The renderer clamps the sheet, insets cell UVs, uses sRGB colors, mip filtering and modest anisotropy, and never mirrors the formations. No atlas is required: a runtime-drawn modular sheet preserves all placements and parallax. Generated art is delivered in chat for owner review/upload and stays out of Git.

**Io · Caldera** (`?level=io`) is a **ten-screen / 100-second production route** with eighteen Skipper patrols and five Crucible crossings, repeat drops of every wing accessory, an eight-second camera approach, and the **Caldera Engine** fight. Select it in the launch menu; Frostwake remains the default.

Crucibles are heavy soot-black spherical carriers with ceramic cheeks and opening pouring valves. They cross from alternating sides, visibly charge the valve for 0.7 seconds, and release three aimed molten droplets every 2.6 seconds. Droplets cool into dark, cracked slag over 0.9 seconds and slow down; both hot and cold slag can be shot apart. Carriers have 10 HP and award 300 points, slag has 1 HP and awards 10. The first and third carriers drop a cannon and Omega respectively. Every weapon can destroy both, including laser, piercing Omega, Towline and Pulse. Their models and cooling shaders are pooled and prewarmed; both appear in the Vite workshop. Existing reviewed textures remain optional.

The route develops from sulfur outskirts (0–20 s), through extraction terraces (20–40 s), processing yards (40–60 s), and dense refinery clusters (60–78 s), to a molten breach (78–90 s) and the broken caldera rim (90–100 s). Its layout follows the reviewed progression mockups: domed pressure vessels on grated, supported terraces, branching insulated pipes, quarry conveyors, furnaces, ruined towers, a damaged pipe gantry across the breach, then cable winches and a cooled lava spill at the rim. The basalt corridor makes broad S-bends between steep sulfur-stained banks, with machinery, pipe branches and conveyors following the changing route. All structures remain below the aircraft; scenery is decorative and cannot block, damage or reward the player.

Conveyor ribs scroll at the same speed as their jagged yellow sulfur loads. Transfer yards occupy gaps between vessel clusters, with reserved clearance around the belts and terminals that also excludes large rocks. Filled feeder hoppers cover the loading positions; covered receiving bins conceal the final drop and loop reset. Furnace vents pulse. Registered burner jets use a two-piece view-aligned plane: an always-rounded lower half at the mouth, and an upper half crossfading from a rising side flame to a matching semicircular cap near overhead. Both halves share looping turbulence and meet at one diameter. Camera-facing smoke puffs follow bounded ballistic arcs from the actual exhaust mouths. Mip-filtered thermal detail flows across both the molten breach and caldera lava, beneath separate cooled crust slabs and glowing fracture walls. These effects all use simulation time, freeze when paused or at results, and restart from the same initial state. They allocate no new geometry or textures during play.

A settled-dust pass adds sulfur powder and loose grains beneath the conveyor transfer points, dusty service aprons, and darker fallout around refinery clusters. Deposits conform to the terrain; dust and irregular vertical grime remain fixed to painted and metal surfaces as the scene scrolls and turns. The PBR coating dulls reflections and raises roughness while preserving furnace emissions and target markings. Chimney mouths have charred metal, exhaust is muted gray-brown, and low powder puffs drift close to the transfer yards. All masks and grains are generated at runtime, with no new uploaded assets or collision changes.

The continuous 3D heightfield uses denser, irregular triangulation along the banks and sparse sampling farther away, divided into culled chunks with identical boundary heights and normals. Broken talus and half-buried fragments roughen the shoulders. Blended Cartesian albedo/bump projections prevent concentric cap mapping on silos and rocks; the silo paint band occupies its own segment of one tapered shell, avoiding overlapping panels. Rock volumes and rubble are instanced, fixed industrial details are merged per assembly/material, and animated ore, jets and smoke use shared instanced batches. The world-space shadow sun stays anchored to the terrain texel grid through scrolling and camera pitch. Lava/furnace fill and a finite HDR reflection probe give machinery warm reflected light. Everything is created and prewarmed before launch.

The boss and its six foot foundations share that same world. Perspective is active from launch. Stage flight keeps a fixed oblique angle and a straight heading, with no path-following pan, bank or pitch sway. Aircraft controls and weapon positions stay anchored to the screen. The ship then coasts to a hover while the view blends smoothly into the existing 35° boss framing above the ground, with no scenic image replacement or boss reveal fade. The 72-HP **Caldera Engine** retains its eight-scoop dredging wheel, locked three-shot volleys and support-break sequence: the first 20 damage breaks the cyan coupling, articulates one failed leg and sags the chassis while the feet remain fixed. A shutter opens over the central machinery for the remaining 52 HP. All ship, drone, wing, Towline and Pulse weapons work in both phases; projectile origins, impact markers and laser contacts follow the posed target.

Vite dev offers **Launch at transition**, the **Io camera study** (`/io-study.html`) with a 0–108 s timeline and section jump menu, failed-support inspection, and Skipper/Crucible/slag/Caldera/burner asset-workshop entries. Production has no QA hooks or skip controls. The owner's reviewed basalt and ceramic maps are optional; finite mip-filtered procedural textures preserve the full route and combat if media is absent, missing or corrupt. Generated media continues to go through chat for owner review/upload.

**Titan · Research station** (`?level=titan`) uses a ten-screen ground plate and begins its ascent at 84 seconds, six seconds before the end of the original ground route. Twenty insect encounters carry repeat drops of all four wing accessories. Smaller four-winged cicadas fly in coherent chevrons, mirrored crossing sweeps, and staggered accelerating dives. Five centipedes grow from six to eight individually damageable, overlapping body sections along the route. Destroying a middle section leaves two connected fragments that turn smoothly and scurry toward opposite sides; cut ends expose a dark joint rather than growing another head. Surviving sections retain their weapon targets, health, and drops. Further cuts, ramming, ship/drone fire, homing missiles, piercing omega shots, continuous lasers, Towline, and Pulse all use the same section hitboxes. Only intact centipede heads shoot; fleeing fragments stop firing and leave the playfield. The patrol models share geometry/materials and are pooled and warmed before launch, including severed ends, so encounters need no new media or first-use GPU uploads. A seven-second ascent follows the patrol: the station and layered horizontal cloud surfaces share a world-space height/pitch transform as the ship climbs and pitches into a face-on fight with **Titan Cicada**. The boss physically rises ahead and enters the camera frustum without a reveal fade or depth slide. Mirrored ground extensions and procedural side ridges cover the exposed terrain; peaks project through the haze during the fight. The three cloud surfaces have moving geometric relief, slope-based lighting, and denser tessellation near the flight corridor. Relief tapers at the distant horizon and stays below the lowest boss-fight aircraft position. The reference-informed head has a broad dark brow, smaller nub-mounted eyes, a downward-pointing ocellar triangle clear of the lower face, a continuous pear-shaped clypeus with swept ribs and a central seam, and a short folded rostrum. Its four translucent wings have veins clipped to their actual membrane triangles, its compound-eyed head sways, and six spined legs move while alternating cyan thorax vents charge locked three-shot volleys. The boss has 80 HP, warning rings before firing, and supports every drone/wing weapon and laser contact/shield effect. In Vite dev, **Launch at transition** skips the patrol and starts at the beginning of the ascent. The uploaded `public/encounters/titan-research-ground-10screens.png` scrolls once, from the outer sampling grounds at its bottom to the disrupted core at its top; the central route remains intact, with mirrored extensions covering the surrounding ground during ascent. Selected ground lamps blink independently and cyan contents move through the glass pipe. The river stays static. Stronger drifting amber haze, windblown dust, and cooling exhaust from registered machinery vents add motion above the ground. An optional four-part raster atlas supplies maintenance crawlers with rigidly rotating survey turrets and two-link tool arms. Crawlers stay hidden until their reviewed atlas is uploaded; gameplay and the remaining scenery work without it. These elements are decorative; they cannot damage, block, or reward the player. Atmosphere stays below the aircraft and combat graphics, and all motion freezes with the simulation. Animation masks are built in-engine from feature coordinates and the decoded artwork, so there is no mask asset to upload. Preflight slices the tall plate into ten screen-sized textures with neighboring filtering gutters, then uploads them before launch. A procedural ground/river/station fallback preserves the same playable route when the optional plate is missing or fails to load. The PWA caches the owner-uploaded plate for offline runs.

Titan's three enemies have separate palettes: jade patrol cicadas with warm eyes and pale green wings, rust-red centipedes with amber plate rims and turquoise eyes, and an ivory boss with wine-dark eyes and a heavy cream/taupe coat. Patrol wings attach to dorsal hinges and their full beat stays above the carapace. Shells use a shared mip-filtered chitin albedo/bump map rather than periodic fragment patterns; the compound eyes no longer use a screen-space facet grid. Short tapered modeled bristles replace the soft fur cards; the boss carries substantially denser stubble than the patrol. Patrol shells use muted gray-green pigment with irregular stains and varied texture coordinates. The optional chitin map has a seeded mip-filtered fallback. The earlier setae PNG is retained as owner artwork but is no longer used. Titan blends continuously from orthographic to perspective projection, coasts the ground to rest, keeps terrain visibility continuous, and preserves the boss animation clock through ascent. No combat or hitbox changes depend on these visuals.

Choose a level before launch. **Frostwake · Ice field** is the default: a ten-screen route with a 90-second traversal, twenty authored waves, repeat weapon drops, and the mosasaur boss **Leviathan**. Ichthyosaur hunting packs and four-flipper plesiosaur sweepers replace the borrowed scout/raptor roster. Hunters alternate weaving chevrons, paired pincers, delayed slalom trains and five-member lines that complete a full loop before diving out; later loops reverse direction. The ten sections move through open drift, ice passages, survey wreckage, and a clear boss arena. The sector readout shows progress from 1/10 to 10/10. Use `?level=ice` to select it directly.

The hunters have shallow lateral nostrils near the beak base, scaleless, countershaded bodies and bank to reveal their pale flanks before accelerating. Plesiosaurs keep a substantial neck, a steady torso, coordinated four-flipper strokes with embedded shoulder/hip roots, transverse stripes, smooth body skin, localized flipper scales, and no dorsal fin; they release a slower five-shot mouth fan. Their long bodies have their own projectile/contact bounds, and authored carriers still release wing accessories. Leviathan has a muscular body with irregular mottled spots, small scale detail, lateral tail propulsion, a vertical two-lobed fluke, and an articulated jaw. The angular skull has raised cranial/brow ridges and forward-facing recessed eyes behind protective orbital rims. A single connected blubber mesh runs from the torso through the neck into the skull and chin; the ventral neck stretches smoothly with the hinged jaw. The socket backs extend into the skull. Lit, muted red flesh covers the palate, throat and jaw-bound tongue; aperture-dependent cavity occlusion approximates light blocked by the roof and cheeks, keeping the deep maw dark while exposing warmer flesh at the lip and tongue. Subdued bone-colored teeth frame the bite. Gentle head yaw, pitch and roll reveal the sides during the fight; the mouth marker and laser contact follow the posed head. Its 60-HP fight uses a central mouth target and locked, alternating horizontal/vertical three-shot volleys with a longer dodge interval. The jaw, charge glow, mouth marker, hit feedback, and laser contact follow the posed rig. Ordinary ship fire remains continuous. The marine projectiles are amber ice-like pellets; these attacks and survival in space are fictional.

The models are procedural skinned meshes with shared geometry and materials. Body and flipper lofts have closed ends, including the plesiosaur nose and tail. Hunter facing, bank, muzzle positions and capsule hit bounds follow their formation paths through sideways and reversed turns. Bone-driven tails, necks, head and flippers are deterministic and pause with the flight. Laser contact tests the posed vertices rather than the bind-pose silhouette. Every pooled model and skeleton buffer is primed before launch; animated bone matrices stream into existing GPU storage during play. The dev workshop includes the boss and patrol cicadas, a centipede with a scrubbed middle-section break and opposite escapes, hunter, sweeper, and Leviathan with animation scrubbing and orbit views.

Anatomy follows the earlier mockups, informed by [scaleless skin, blubber and countershading in *Stenopterygius*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0775-x), [smooth tail skin and small flipper scales in one Jurassic plesiosaur](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2025.01.001), and [a preserved bilobed mosasaur tail fin](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms3423). These specimens inform the designs; exact colors, scale distribution beyond preserved areas, behavior and proportions remain artistic interpretations.

Frostwake is assembled from six shared procedural asteroid shapes, wreckage modules, and anchored mining stations. Irregular fracture planes retain sharp rims while worn regions and UV seams share smooth normals. Periodic cellular normal and roughness maps add angular joints, tilted ice faces, and chipped frost. A nonmetallic physical material uses water ice's 1.31 refractive index, with a shared procedural HDR lighting probe for white specular glints. Fake refraction bends the same cropped, rotating galaxy behind the clear faces; frosted joints scatter more light. Three bounded half-resolution depth layers let clear ice refract other asteroids behind it. Stable body IDs reject self-refraction and depth tests reject foreground leakage. Capture colors retain sky transmission without recursive framebuffer feedback; actors and combat effects stay out of the buffers. Overlaps deeper than the captured layers fall back to the galaxy. The relief maps and lighting probe are included in preflight warm-up. Background rocks, solid outcrops, and foreground rocks have distinct real depths. Free asteroids have independent visible tumbles (0.07–0.18 radians per second) about arbitrary 3D axes; only outcrops with mining stations and attitude jets hold a fixed lane orientation. Small survey huts give way to weathered gantries, container decks, refineries, and docks as the route progresses. Metal surfaces carry procedural grime and minor corrosion. During the boss approach, the field pitches into a 3D corridor along the same forward axis as the fighter and its wing pods. Leviathan paces the ship as rocks and industry stream out of the distance throughout the fight. Decorative rocks recycle beyond the viewport so the pursuit never runs out of scenery; solid stations pass only once and the arena stays clear. A one-and-a-half-screen galactic plate fills the distant sky and reorients during the camera turn. Repeat-aware edge blending hides wrap seams and covers every viewport corner. Nothing uses a mirrored or repeating long backdrop. Pale outcrops with paired amber markers obstruct the fighter, drone, and wing pods without damaging hull; the central escape lane stays open and the last screen has no solid outcrops. Enemy paths stay clear of the solids. Dark distant rocks and unmarked foreground fragments are decorative. Layout is deterministic and shared with collision, so optional media cannot change the route.

**Broodlock · Ant hive** keeps its prototype: a 28-second traversal of the three-screen hive strip, six waves of infected ants, then a brood guardian. Workers fire aimed spores; larger soldiers charge their amber vents before a three-spore fan. The guardian uses the existing alternating hardpoint, warning-ring, and volley mechanics. The procedural ants have textured chitin, segmented abdomens, elbowed antennae, serrated mandibles, and a jointed six-leg gait. Soldiers have broader heads and larger jaws. Their eyes are cloudy and pale, a long fruiting stalk emerges from the head, and branching parasite colonies and crust spread across their bodies. Five soft mold sprites coat the abdomen, thorax, and head. These planes always face the camera, including while orbiting in the workshop, and leave the amber attack vents readable.

Procedural mushrooms attach to fixed coordinates on the hive strip. Their mycelial roots and contact shadows meet the painted pipes and rock, while tapered stems bend sideways and return toward the camera. Caps follow the direction of each neck, with modeled rims and underside gills. The hive uses a shallow perspective view so the caps have foreground parallax while their roots stay on the same source pixels throughout scrolling and the boss approach.

Stems and caps obstruct the fighter, drone, and wing pods. Contact slides craft along the growth without damaging hull. A blocked touch target releases immediately; a blocked drone parks at the surface, and Glint's Hot Dock counts the actual completed move. Scrolling growth pushes a parked craft clear, including at screen edges. The central flight lane remains open. Collision shapes use the same curves, cap profiles, and projection as the models, and do not depend on texture files.

Ants look ahead along their path and steer into clear lanes before reaching a fruiting body. They bank gently during detours and ease back toward their original lane afterward. Collision clearance also keeps their bodies and legs out of stems and caps. Their avoidance is identical with art and placeholders.

**Overgrown preserve** keeps the original four waves and draconian interceptor fight. Its second wave includes a feathered raptor whose wings fold before a dive. Use `?level=preserve` to open that level directly.

The procedural encounter rigs live in `src/encounter-rigs.ts`. A future model can use the same local pose and pivot names while the simulation owns movement, attack timing, and hitboxes. The interceptor's `muzzle_L` and `muzzle_R` pivots line up with the existing cannon lanes. All bosses also expose `laser_contact_L` and `laser_contact_R` anchors on their visible muzzle faces for cosmetic laser contact.

## Assets and offline play

The entire run plays with procedural shapes and works without media files. Generated media goes through chat review and is uploaded by the owner. See [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

Run `npm run build` and serve `dist/` over HTTPS for a deployment. Use `npm run preview` for a local production check. Once the title screen reports **Ready offline**, reload offline and play a run. The standalone manifest picks up the owner-supplied `public/icons/spaceslop-192.png` and `public/icons/spaceslop-512.png` when present. Supply both for Chrome's install promotion.

Choose Vex, Brass, or Glint on the title or results screen. The selected pilot is remembered on the device. On desktop, the portrait, perk status, and flight controls sit beside the playfield.

- **Vex · Close Call** grants one extra Pulse after four enemy-shot grazes per stage.
- **Brass · Field Repair** restores one hull the first time you replace an equipped wing weapon while damaged.
- **Glint · Hot Dock** doubles the first Sentry shot or Towline pulse after the drone completes a move of at least three playfield units. The perk cools down for three seconds after the attack.

To install the optional pilot art, place square PNGs at `public/pilots/vex.png`, `public/pilots/brass.png`, and `public/pilots/glint.png`, then rebuild. The build precaches the portraits it finds. Missing or unreadable images fall back to callsign tiles.

The encounter textures and backdrops are optional PNGs. Add the reviewed files and rebuild to precache them for offline play. The workshop also shows the model textures.

Without a mask, a simple central-channel split is used. New hive geometry and textures join the preflight warm-up so the first ant and guardian do not trigger their initial uploads during play.

Ant and mushroom surfaces retain deterministic code-drawn color, bump, and roughness detail without the chitin and fungus albedo files. Those two optional PNGs replace color only; keep them opaque and do not import them as normal maps. The mold patch instead needs alpha, and has a procedural fuzzy fallback when absent or unreadable. Its sprites share materials, blend over the shell, and do not write depth. With no hive foreground, procedural side rails meet the same mushroom roots; positions and collision remain identical. The prototype strip always spans three screens. Shared mushroom, parasite, and mold rendering is warmed before launch, including the variants farther up the strip. Layout and collision live in `src/hive-layout.ts` and `src/hive-collision.ts`, ant avoidance in `src/ant-navigation.ts`, meshes in `src/hive-fungi.ts`, `src/ant-rigs.ts`, and `src/ant-parasite.ts`, and mold sprites in `src/ant-mold.ts`; no model download is required.

Frostwake art is delivered through chat for owner upload. The two surface textures and galactic backdrop are optional and are precached when present at build time. Without them, modeled frost colors, procedural surface relief, a procedural galactic sky, and 3D distant rocks preserve the scene. Normal/roughness detail and metal patina are generated in-engine, requiring no additional uploaded maps. Wrecks reuse the existing optional metal texture. New scenery geometry, materials, refinery/station buffers, and textures join preflight warm-up before launch; modules farther along the route are warmed too.
